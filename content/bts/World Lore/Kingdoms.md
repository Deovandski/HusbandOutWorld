# Documentation Notation

Religion isn't a thing in Vraki: it's an agnostic world, built on science and soul energy. There are no churches or priests, no "hell", no witches and no curses. Each kingdom's Customs section lists traditions, not religion. Cults do exist, just like bandit groups. (World Lore Q&A › Vol 4 Draft 2, Q6.)

# Freeish (Freeishan)

What were small villages became interconnected. Each village ruled itself, but villages gathered around shared values. Red tape between village groups grew until you could spend days at a village border just to get through, all because the village elder woke up on the wrong foot. People started rebelling, so the powers that be in most villages got together and created what amounts to a governor, who defuses issues so the village groups can trade as easily as possible. It's a fake power to keep people in check, but it did cut the red tape: several elected governors were smart and charming, while the less charming ones resorted to blackmail to keep village leaders in line. The village leaders, in turn, keep the governors in check so their power doesn't run rampant.

With the Prosperity Treaty, the decentralized villages became a kingdom in their own right, as an exchange of favors between the Freeish village leaders and each kingdom's courtroom scientists, so they could keep the same village leaders and governor system.

"FoodShare" is an actual job: people who carry food between villages, like food delivery, since Blood villages don't really make Soil village food and vice versa. Real-world comparison: Mumbai's dabbawalas, who deliver home-cooked lunchboxes across the city.

![][image1]![][image2]

There are two village groups:

- **Blood Group:** lives on the outskirts and relies more on hunting than farming.
- **Soil Group:** lives in the middle and relies more on farming and fishing.

![][image3]

#### Royalty and politics

A democratic approach. When a village leader dies, the residents hold a mass vote to elect the next one, and the current leader's sons and daughters can't be voted in. It went a bit sideways, though: for the last few generations, the vote in many villages has been between the four most powerful (non-royal) guilds of each group. When the governor dies, the village leaders elect the next one, with the Deltacross villages joining in through backdoor votes.

- **Village leader:** the right hand is tattooed with a green circle, a painful process to remind them the job must be taken seriously.
- **Governor:** the right side of the face is tattooed, kind of like below.

Healing (Body Restore or Nari Refresher) removes the tattoos, so they have to be re-inked.

![][image4]

#### Relationships and threats

It's a small political threat to the other kingdoms if it gets further ahead than they do. The Blood and Soil groups have started fighting.

#### Society

Mostly humans, with some Cruros, Wozefaries and Sorefaries. Almost no Darfaries, who prefer Blood City for the hunting. The Blood groups tend to have more people prone to fighting.

#### Customs and culture

Their customs revolve around fauna, flora, beasts and nature in general: basically a mix of other beliefs. There's a sense that one's own village and village group are better than the others, but people are very warm within their group. The Soil group protects the land more and is more in touch with the Trunk People, while the Blood group tends to be against them. People often say "meet the darkness" instead of "death". Real-world comparison: India, for its business style and wacky city layouts.

#### Daily life

- **Clothing:** exposed skin, for warmer weather. White clothing with accents or embroidery in other colors. Soil clothing is looser, for farming; Blood clothing is closer to adventurer or guard clothing.
- **Education:** similar to Forway, but each village group customizes the clothing and has different priorities: hunting versus farming.
- **Crime and law:** set by each village, though they aren't very different. Some follow Forway Law; some switch laws every election, depending on how disorganized they are. The Blood groups' laws tend to be closer to each other.
- **Guards:** Freeish guards are like a dollar-store version of Forway guards, but their courage and drive to protect their kingdom more than make up for their lighter, versatile bone-wood armor. When they pull their short swords from their forearm sleeves, get some distance, unless you can parry and strike true like no other. They like their nari bombs to close the gap, too. And their reflexes seem higher than average: the whole Freeish bloodline of guards has a tradition of training on hallucinogens from childhood, to sharpen their awareness and reaction times.
- **Local embassy quests:** Ganyrami bounty hunting, the nari stone transport team.
- **Economy:** farming many kinds of flora, plus hunting for the Blood groups. Trade with Blood, Ganyrami, and Forway through Deltacross.

#### Guilds

- **Gyut Farming Guild (#13):** a big farming guild that controls several Gyut farms.
- **Greenbread Farming Guild (#14):** the same, for Greenbread.
- **Two Hearts (#15):** a guild of adventurers from the region, famous for having Fernan Blood as a member. Named after the Bagarisa, which has two hearts.

#### Marriage

It varies by village, but usually a proposal is made with a bundle of fruit, in front of everyone in the village plaza. If she accepts, they marry in front of the village leader, who says: "Just like your feelings for one another, plant these seeds in a home filled with love and care." If both are residents of the village, they get a guild for free if they want to start one; if they're from different Freeish villages, the cost is split; if one isn't from Freeish, they pay their own costs.

#### Names

Names in this democratic kingdom sound like Brazilian names, and influences from other kingdoms' names and cultures are common, given its history. People rarely have Freeish as their city name; they use one of the villages below.

#### Points of interest

- **Governor State (#1):** the main house, where the current elected leader lives. Not as big as a whole village, but the whole state covers about 13 km² (about 5 square miles) of a private island where all the elected kingdom personnel live and work.
- **Carbeyland (#2):** the "original" village, some say, where Carbey was first planted en masse. The other Carbeyland (#11) says the same: both call themselves the original, the first true village that founded what is now Freeish, and they resent each other to this day.
- **Farplace (#3):** it was, and still is, kind of far from everything.
- **Treetop (#4):** named after the area's hunters, who often attack prey from the treetops.
- **The Mill (#5):** the name stuck after it became the center of water-powered grain milling.
- **Big Gyut (#6):** named after a farmer who harvested an oversized Gyut that could fill a Darfaries stomach... somehow.
- **Sweet Siguash (#7):** its people were the first to plant Siguash en masse.
- **Peaceful Life (#8):** in the middle of the other villages, so beasts rarely prey on it.
- **Endland (#9):** named for being close to the water.
- **Crossroads (#10):** the first village people fleeing Sanbi or Tolerideme would see, where they decided where to go next.
- **Carbeyland (#11):** the other "original" village where Carbey was first planted en masse (see Carbeyland #2).
- **Lishies Eater (#12):** the oldest village of the bunch, from around the fall of Ganyrami. Legend says the people here hunted down and ate every Lishie that survived the fall.

#### Festivals

- **Village Competition:** held in Paulin 998 A.G., but a major fight broke out, so it was never held again. People are stirring the pot to have it again.
- **Blood & Soil:** in the fall, people put their differences aside, exchange gifts between the village groups and party until sunrise. Distant families gather too, to trade knowledge, but mostly to banter once too much alcohol is involved.
- **White Roundies Arrival:** every winter the White Roundies arrive, and people party with them, since they behave like dogs but are the size of a human. People also try to trade them food for things they find in the deep sea: sometimes they get lucky and get gold, sometimes a half-eaten fish.

#### Burial

Burial in the fields or woods is common. People tell their families where they want to be buried, and a stone with Vraki writing is placed over their resting place.

#### Visual references

Lots of trees and fields. Mostly smaller houses, spread out, among big old trees and lots of green, with some Skyrim and Japanese influence, built with Bone Wood. The village leader's and some richer people's houses are two stories tall. Trunk People serve as light sources on the big old Soul Wood trees along the roads. Some really large flowers here and there, which people grow for the floral scent they give off.

# Gandew (Gandewian)

Gandew Mountain is about 6 km tall. You have to travel above ground to Sundeath: underground, monsters mess with the tunnels, and people would probably get lost.

#### Royalty and politics

No royal markings: everyone should know who the leader is. The king is decided by challenging the previous king to a duel at King Fest; whoever rips out both of the previous king's tusks becomes the new king. If the defeated king has a daughter, he must give her away in marriage. A king's sons can't challenge their father, to prevent despotism and quid pro quo. The king is also the courtroom scientist, unless he appoints someone else (if he wants to, or cares just enough about Soul Energy). There's no queen: women can't fight for the king's spot.

#### Society

Only Darfaries may live in the Gandew Kingdom permanently; Wozefaries and Sorefaries are thrown off the Goodbye Cliff. (Wozefaries can dampen the fall by activating the Darfaries interface as an antigravity spell, and Sorefaries can fly.) For Gandew, perfection is being Darfaries. All visitors are welcome.

#### Customs and culture

They worship the ancient Darfaries who took up the sword and cleansed the mountain of the other species. They believe all spirits live forever, and get lost in the desert if their bodies don't go through the proper rituals (from visions of people hallucinating in the heat).

They get along with other species but keep their privacy; they rarely make friends, but when they do, it's for life. They avoid using Soul Energy for anything they can do by hand, so they don't grow dependent on it, since the ancient Darfaries cleaned the mountain with their bare hands (not quite 100% true). That runs through the rest of their society, including their reputation for preserved, non-refrigerated food. They greet with the Mountain Greeting, which Hiromi teaches Harry in Vol 2: hands up and feet spread apart, they come belly to belly and hand to hand, spreading their feet wider (or lowering a hand) to level with someone shorter. Communists, and very clean people despite the sand. Boots and sandals stay in one spot inside the home, like in Japanese homes.

**The Blood Book:** families gather in the spring, and villages in the fall. The head of the family or village opens the book and reads out the expectations and promises made last time for each signee. The signee says what they succeeded at, what they failed at, and what the new expectations will be. Meanwhile, anyone else may promise something to the signee, or challenge them to a duel: to bash some sense into their head, for failing to do something they promised, or to settle bad blood between them. Once reviewed, the signee signs in blood, making them responsible for what they stated. When everyone has been reviewed, the bookkeeper signs in blood, seals the book, and everyone feasts to celebrate life.

On the street, lots of one-person vendors sell food and drinks; they also sell in the depths of the dungeons inside Gandew, beneath the sand, where beasts dig tunnels. A party of Darfaries usually has a "bard" who serves drinks and plays music to keep the party (a literal party, partying by slaying beasts) going.

#### Daily life

- **Clothing:** warmer clothing for life at high altitude. Overdressed, on the threatening side, but it also helps cross the desert by reflecting sunlight.
- **Education:** Darfaries children get many free things, including fundamental education and Wooly Gyut Milk, but they're heavily trained in the Gandew way of life.
- **Crime and law:** every punishment is settled by combat, under the Gandew Common Duel Rights & Law, first implemented around 375 A.G. (see [Laws & Treaties](/lore/laws-and-treaties/)).
- **Guards:** Gandew guards are the most physical, and they're brutal about it. If their blunt weapon touches you, you likely won't be going anywhere for a while. They tend to travel alone, by custom, but you'd be foolish to think they're easy pickings: their chitin armor is tough, and they earn their pay with the blood and tears of their enemies. Don't expect much conversation while their helmet is on: they're more reserved than other guards, and the helmet locks their large tusks in place, so they can't open their mouths. Some say, "Pfff, it was a design flaw with the armor, but they won't admit it, because Darfaries being Darfaries, am I right?" Scholars have proven it was made to create silent servitude, though: a feature, not a bug.
- **Local embassy quests:** desert protection and clearing sand off the roads; maintaining the mining holes and diving deeper; exploring and maintaining the desert and the west and north.
- **Economy:** trades Wooly milk, wool and Wooly beef jerky with the other kingdoms (especially Sanbi), and Domar Carbey from Tolerideme. Also grows Rakuja, though less of it, for lack of space. Mints Sapphire and Aquamarine balls. Minerals and blacksmithing.

#### Guilds

- **Tough Hammer (#9):** a guild famous for tough warriors who keep their emotions in check: calmer, but able to land a precise punch every single time.

#### Marriage

When two Darfaries like each other, they duel to see if it ends in a draw. If it does, they mate for life.

#### Names

Names often lean toward Asian names, especially Chinese and Japanese. City names are usually "High-Gandew", "Medium-Gandew" or "Low-Gandew". For all intents and purposes, that's the law of the land, and you'll likely be ostracized if it isn't on your badge. The culture has relaxed a bit, though: people shorten "Medium-Gandew" to "Mid-Gandew" on badges and get away with it, and at this rate "Mid-Gandew" may become official with the guards and kingdom staff.

#### Points of interest

- **Central Farm (#2):** where local crops, Rakuja included, are grown most intensively.
- **Central Herd (#3):** where most Woolies live, and most meat and milk is produced.
- **Grand Hall (#4):** where the current king and his family live.

#### Festivals

- **Weapon Fest:** every Adrian, to see who makes the toughest weapon in each category.
- **King Fest:** every Asa, a major trial by combat among everyone who thinks they're worthy of the king's position. In ordinary fights, for fun or to the death, everyone is healed before every fight, and the loser has to "lick his wounds", or get someone to lick them. Challenging the king for the throne is different: there can be several challengers, so the king is healed between all their fights, but the challengers aren't. That stops an endless torrent of challenges from forcing the king off his throne by sheer attrition, breaking his spirit instead of beating him in a fair fight. The winner faces the king, and must rip out both of his tusks after killing him.

#### Burial

Cremation. The bones are washed with sand from the mountain, then stored in an urn with the ashes for the family to keep.

#### Visual references

Mud and cave houses in the mountain. Fost stones are used inside homes, while public places mostly rely on fire for light and heat, as tradition demands.

# Sanbi (Sanbian)

![][image37]

It was once a fishing village that grew into a semi-ordered city. It's named after the Sanbi fish, which only fishermen from this area could catch, and the other kingdoms visit to enjoy the infinite variety of dishes made from the treasured fish.

#### Royalty and politics

A kingdom, but with power split among four players in three departments:

- **Monarchy Department:** the King handles foreign affairs (trade deals, watching over the Prosperity Treaty, etc.); the Queen handles local and social affairs (education, urban planning).
- **Soul Energy Department:** the Courtroom Scientist handles security (investigations, guards, etc.).
- **Economic Department:** the King-Maid handles economic affairs and bookkeeping, oversees the castle maids, and implements deals and writes up their minutes. Frugality is the name of the game. Eiri always meets Sora on Tollday and Sannday to catch up on the incoming flood of paperwork.

The department meeting is held every Freeday, or more often if needed. Like a standup, it brings together Neferteri, Itagiba, Eiri and Kibe: the King-Maid pre-writes the topics, reads them out and records the votes, and the Queen dismisses the meeting. Any of the four can be expelled if the other three agree, and any can retire whenever they want.

**Royal markings:** blue strands of hair.

#### Relationships and threats

The Cruros, if they get too big; some Sanbi citizens still hold a grudge against them for taking away the easy job of collecting pearls. And the True Sanbi King, if the fish breaks the deal they have.

#### Society

Mostly humans, with a few Wozefaries, Sorefaries and Cruros.

#### Customs and culture

See Novels › The True Sanbi King. They pray to the sea every day so it doesn't swallow their fishermen, and their burial rituals are closely tied to giving back what they took from the sea: "Our soul is tied to the sea."

Sanbi people are closer to Brazilians on a random weekend than to ancient Greeks: news spreads as bar talk, not forums. They live for emotional sustenance: give in to what your heart desires. Very handsy, frugal, warm and friendly; somewhat sloppy and careless, not as intelligent as people from Forway, but often with more street smarts. Not many festivals, since people celebrate their daily lives and live in the present, but lots of weekend parties. A Sanbi rave can go on so long, with so much booze, that people get trampled to death and are revived once the party's over. Their gesture for "you're fucked" or "you're done": hands and arms flat in front, closing three times like a Sanbi's mouth.

**Sanbi Castle:** no marble; the walls are a fine white plaster, common across the Sanbi Kingdom. There's no war room, but there's a bar (Vol 2, Chapter 1) and the Monarchy office, where Neferteri prefers to negotiate. The castle maids are paid very well and it feels like family, so few walk away.

#### Daily life

- **Clothing:** fish leather is everywhere. Exposed skin; sailor bandanas are common.
- **Education:** not required, but not paid for by the kingdom either. Fewer children go than in other kingdoms, and overall intelligence is slightly lower.
- **Crime and law:** follows Forway Law. Minor punishments are paid by replacement. The major punishment is being thrown into the sea; if the criminal survives, the sea absolves them of their crime.
- **Guards:** Sanbi guards are flexible: they use knives, nari gels and nari bombs to deal with a nuisance quickly, or to get the fuck away if it's too much for them. Their large bows, combined with nari gels, give them varied fighting techniques: they can be sneakier, build more muscle, or poison their arrows with debilitating nari gels. There's a reason their training stresses an "everything is a weapon" mindset, and so should you, if you feel like disturbing the peace of their kingdom.
- **News:** official announcements are made by gathering people for an event around Sanbi Castle, or wherever suits the occasion: everything is an excuse to get together. Otherwise, the news goes to the guards, and people just ask a guard or a village elder. Not much is posted on walls; when something must be, like a wanted poster, the kingdom uses the embassy's services, for convenience.
- **Local courier:** the kingdom's own courier delivers anywhere in the kingdom for a flat 1 Pearl. It's not fast, since no Wingas are used (in emergencies, a human turns into a Large Winga and carries the message). Letters are one-way: if the address is wrong or the person isn't found, the mail goes to the castle, where it's stored for a while before being thrown out. Same postcard size as Winga mail, but the envelope is always white, to avoid confusion with Winga mail, and FROM and TO are written in uppercase.

  ![][image41]

- **Local embassy quests:** Crocus control, Cruro reconnaissance, Aldemeri bounties, Newland exploration, overseeing the sea.
- **Economy:** exports Sanbi fish products, such as dried fish and clothing made with Sanbi scales; fish meal to the Gandew and Tolerideme Kingdoms for farming; and fish oil. Trades with the Cruros. Some Fost stone collection, and salt production.

#### Guilds

- **Half-Cruros (#6):** Kiko, a highly intelligent Cruro who left Cruro land, created this guild for himself and every other Cruro who left to live a decent life. It also helps new Cruros who have just fled.
- **Sanbi Royal:** every member governing the city (and their sons and daughters) is in this guild.

#### Marriage

The tradition is to declare your love loud and clear in front of the Sanbi Fish fountain. If they accept, they share a passionate kiss and hug.

#### Names

Sanbi names are often the most unique, and unlike in other kingdoms, you can change yours as often as you like. So much so that the Sanbi Royals have a special subsidy contract with Forway to lower the cost of badge remakes for their citizens, while Forway officials are still dealing with the aneurysm-inducing problem of tracking previous names tied to each soul signature without ballooning costs. Names often sound like Brazilian Indigenous or Indian names. Sanbians like cool, distinct names, especially one that speaks to their soul: the kind that makes you want to scream it in the middle of the street on a stormy night, or the one you proudly repeat over and over because nobody can fucking understand how to write or say it.

#### Festivals

- **The Sanbi Fish Parade:** every Adrian, people dress up in Sanbi fish costumes to celebrate the end of another year.

#### Burial: the Soul Send Away

A collar of stones gathered from the bottom of the sea is put around the person's neck (if there's no body, their favorite possession is offered instead). A heavy stone is tied to the body, and it's cast into the sea at the spot they wanted, or one their guild chooses. Before it's cast, friends and family usually hold a ceremony to talk about the person. As the body leaves for the ocean, usually from the Old Wharf, people chant: "A person lives from the sea, and so shall the sea live from the body." If no one gathered, the person dropping the body into the sea says it.

# Tolerideme (Toleridemian)

The land was incredibly fertile, but human technology couldn't make the most of it until the three sisters came along and revolutionized farming with Soul Energy and fish meal mixed with human poop. Over time an oligarchy formed, and it taxed alcohol to fund the war, until an uprising broke out. After the revolution, power was split between two people: the Farm Leader and the Alcohol Leader. When a leader dies, a castle party is held for the elite to say goodbye and appoint a new one. The courtroom scientist, a person appointed by Forway City, assists the pair and keeps them in check on the Prosperity Treaty.

#### Royalty and politics

- **Farm Leader** (the Farm King, or Dirt King): carries a wooden shovel on the hip, passed down through the generations. Nothing about it seems special.
- **Alcohol Leader** (the Alcohol King, or Booze King): carries a corkscrew on the hip, also passed down through the generations, and also nothing special, other than being the same one.

#### Relationships and threats

Some Waterfall City residents look down on them for shunning physical work and relying too much on magic. Waterfall residents would also much rather negotiate prices and be flexible, while Toleridemians don't care for negotiating. The Wood People are a threat, but they don't seem to care.

#### Society

Obese and overweight humans are fairly common; the poorest are easier to spot than the rich. Some Darfaries, also overweight; rarely Wozefaries or Sorefaries. Humans are on average shorter than Harry.

#### Customs and culture

They revere the three smart ladies and aim to be as smart, and as fat, as they were. They don't really care what happens after death; they'd rather focus on staying revived as long as possible.

More honest than most, and ironclad: their culture likely won't ever change, since everyone follows it so closely. They don't negotiate: the stated price is the price they've concluded is fairest. They rely heavily on Soul Energy and avoid physical work as much as possible, with a "fair work, fair pay" mindset and a utilitarian view of tools. They're precise: if someone promised something, especially showing up on a given day and time, they'd better. Downing a massive amount of alcohol and keeping it down is considered tough. They wear hats to show their symbol, work, life, etc., and they're louder than most. Toilets are communal so the poop can be collected, and they still mix it with Soul Energy and fish meal.

They love their rituals of social interaction, like the Tamada culture of Georgia (real-world comparison: the [tamada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamada), a toastmaster at a feast). The toastmaster weaves the conversations of the different clusters of people at the table together, bringing a sense of unity and keeping the pace; they have to empty their glass at every toast and hold their drink at bay. Toasting can last several hours, and the toastmaster or any guest uses magic to raise their glass high for a toast.

#### Guilds

- **The 300kg Club (#6):** only Tolerideme citizens weighing more than 300 kg can join. Coincidentally, members don't stay in the club for long.

#### Marriage

There's no marriage. Adults do whatever they want, but if a child is born, both parents must raise it together, by law. Orgies are not uncommon.

#### Names

Their impeccable precision reaches their names too: every name has an even number of letters, often exactly 4 or 6. Why? Because there's no "maybe" and no exceptions: it's what the Smart Ladies did, so it's the law. Names are usually in the English spectrum, from US to UK, with some Australian notes mixed in.

#### Festivals

- **Shake Belly Festival:** three days at the end of Adrian, to cheer everyone up for Asa. Each household cooks on one of the days (a ladle on the door marks the cooking households), while the others go around with decorated bowls, shaking their bellies at people's doors. The better the belly shake, the more food they get.
- **Harvest Festival:** a massive festival in Moacir, right before Adrian. A huge feast is set up on the main streets by the castle, where alcohol makers compete for the best alcohol in each category. The popular-vote winners tend to raise their prices and batch sizes until the next vote.

#### Burial

They cremate the dead with Wood People wood, and use the ashes to fertilize the land they own.
