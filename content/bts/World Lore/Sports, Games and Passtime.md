# Homeegg

A common sport in Forway, played with a nari gel that allows flight.

**Rules:** two teams of five: four attackers on each side, and a fifth player on each team who is the goalkeeper and strategist. The teams take turns fighting in the center (like American football) to advance past the flags. Each turn has five chances to advance the ball and drop it in the "nests" on each pole.

# Fast Fish

A nari gel-powered swimming race, held in a few places but most famous between Waterfall and the Sanbi Kingdom: people compete to see who swims the farthest. Like an olympiad, it shows who has the best body, mind and nari gel.

**Rules:** anything goes, except flight or gravity Soul Energy interfaces.

# Fast Bird

A nari gel-powered flying race, held in a few places but usually in Forway City. Like an olympiad, it shows who has the best body, mind and nari gel.

**Rules:** certain Soul Energy interfaces and nari gels are allowed.

# Fish Catch Fish & Fishing Competitions

Held in a few places, but usually in Sanbi.

**Rules:** Fish Catch Fish is literally fishing with your hands and skills, swimming with the power of nari gels. Depending on the competition, the winner is decided by the size of the catch, the time, or the quantity.

# Gandew Duels

Duels are common between Gandew residents, since that's how they resolve their disputes.

**Rules:** see the Gandew Common Duel Rights & Law on [Laws & Treaties](/lore/laws-and-treaties/). No Soul Energy tampering allowed.

# Territory Snipping

A darts game all about capturing territory and holding onto it.

**Rules:** the board looks like a bird's-eye view of a baseball field rotated into a full circle, split into zones of different colors. Each player gets a bag of darts in their own colors. Everyone throws at the same time, against a set time (until they run out of darts), while a third person keeps track of the territories claimed. Landing on a color region scores a point; land on a region that holds someone else's dart, and the two cancel out. The smaller the region, the more it's worth, and some territories (yellow, for example) are worth more if you snipe them all. Hiromi teaches Harry the game in Vol 2.

# Forway Duel

Like a Gandew duel, but here Soul Energy and nari gels are encouraged. There are no formal rules; it's more of a common saying.

**Rules:** big duels between well-known duelists are held in Forway's theaters for people to watch, and in arenas, sometimes as a team deathmatch.

# Egua Race

Officially held in Forway City.

**Rules:** certain Soul Energy enhancements can be applied to the eguas to make things more entertaining.

# Dueling Egua Race

Officially held in Forway City.

**Rules:** certain Soul Energy enhancements can be applied to the eguas and riders to make things more entertaining. All weapons allowed.

# Soul Energy Art

Officially held in Forway City, but in some other places as well.

**Rules:** the performer uses everything in the Soul Energy interface books to entertain guests creatively. It's also done on the street: someone wowing kids by touching different powers to make different kinds of flames, like a flamethrower or a firework show.

# Alcohol/Food competition

Held in some places; Tolerideme's alcohol competition is famous.

**Rules:** depends on who's hosting, but it's usually annual.

# Stealball

Usually played among friends.

**Rules:** draw a large square on the ground and put an equal number of balls (amethyst or higher) in the center. Then use pearl balls to knock the balls in the center out of the square and steal them. In poorer communities, pearl balls go in the center and small rocks do the stealing.

**Real-world comparison:** much like Ringer, the classic marbles game, where players shoot marbles out of a ring and keep the ones they knock out.

# Flat Steel

More common in Forway: a fancier version of Stealball for richer people who love the game but don't want to be seen playing it on public streets.

![][image3]

**Rules:** like Stealball, but on a table with ground nari stones added, and with three players. A Table Manager applies effects to the table and sets dead zones where the table wins the balls. The players fight each other, or team up against the table; if they team up, the table can make an underhanded proposal, or raise the difficulty while raising the pot. Table powers include bounce, slow, fast and sink zones, splitting a ball, and a hard ball that won't move.

# Wood Hammering

Usually played in taverns.

**Rules:** based on Hammerschlagen (also called Stump), a German game not many people know. Each player starts with a nail half driven into a waist-high stump; on your turn, you get a single swing at your nail with the wedge end of the hammer, and the first to drive theirs flush into the wood wins. Using different types of wood, like Soul Wood or a Rakuja tree, makes it harder.

# Bone Sack

Usually played in taverns.

**Rules:** throw small pieces of bone from a set spot into sacks placed around the room, usually at weird heights or in awkward places. Commonly, you take a big gulp of your drink every time you miss.

# Wood Bowl Spin

Usually played in taverns.

**Rules:** roulette. Everyone usually puts in an equal amount, placing balls in the open slots. The spinner spins the wooden bowl and drops in a white ball; wherever it lands, the spinner gets the prize.

# Minari

It started with carved wooden statues and evolved over time. One statue in a group is the true one (the one with a nari stone inside), and the goal is to eliminate it. The table is carved in 3D, with pieces that plug in and out.

**Rules:** like Gwent, but with carved statues instead of cards. The statues are usually bound with a nari stone, and Soul Energy reflects their powers.

# Music playing/Bands

Music is still medieval, but with some vibes from today's notes and rhythms; no electric guitars or anything like that.

**Real-world comparison:** bardcore (or tavernwave), medieval-style remakes of modern songs played on lutes, pipes, hurdy-gurdies and the like.

# Drinking/Eating/Tavern-Hopping

Getting drunk is fun. Most people have a favorite tavern that becomes a second home, and dedicated restaurants are a strange concept. Some taverns, usually in Forway City, require memberships to keep their standing as high-class establishments. Tavern-hopping is a common trend for adventurers and travelers wanting to taste different things.

# Brothels

Because sticking your dick in, or the reverse, or both, depending on taste, is fun.

# Reading

Novels of old tales float around, usually for richer people who can read.

# Theater

Common in Forway City. Theaters recreate novels or tell entirely different stories.

# Tourism

Usually for adventurers or richer folks: exploring points of interest in different cities and enjoying the scenery.
