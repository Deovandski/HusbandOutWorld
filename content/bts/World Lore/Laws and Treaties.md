# The Prosperity Treaty (Prosperity Law)

The founding treaty, from 680 A.G., when Paulin of Freeish and three courtroom scientists (Asa the Powerful of Gandew, Adrian of North Tolerideme and Bijura Moacir of Sanbi) overthrew their kingdoms to end the Greed Period. It's built on the Sanbi Kingdom's laws of the time, adapted to create Forway: one administrative body between all the kingdoms, to grow trade, steady relations and share resources against threats from outside and within.

In short:

- **Citizens (§1):** a citizen is whoever holds a badge made at a Four Kingdom Embassy. The new Freeish Kingdom, which governs where Ganyrami once stood, is an equal of Sanbi, Tolerideme and Gandew.
- **Travel and adventurers (§2):** every citizen can travel freely through every kingdom and city. Adventuring is a Forway job, run through the embassies, as a decentralized army; adventurers can't pass judgment without a Forway guard's or a kingdom representative's say-so.
- **Money and measures (§3):** no bartering between treaty parties (haggling is fine if the owner allows it); everything is paid in Balls currency; everyone uses the Vraki Calendar and the Vraki units of measure.
- **Contracts (§4):** a contract of near-equal value to both sides is supreme (except for adventurers in a Forway emergency). It's legal with both blood signatures, checkable with a badge, as long as the termination section on the back isn't voided. Slavery is banned, but selling your own freedom for something of similar value is allowed. Abuse is judged by the local kingdom's court, or by Forway law outside a kingdom; forcefully breaking a contract is a crime, for anyone, Forway included.
- **Land and taxes (§5):** Forway owns everything outside the kingdoms, forever, and decides what counts as a village or a city. Forway cities need an embassy and follow the Forway Law; cities pay 20% tax and villages 15%, and Forway sends a battalion to take over if a place's debt reaches 50% of its estimated GDP. Kingdoms rule themselves, but must host a Forway embassy on Forway-controlled land and pay 10%. Embassies can issue emergency calls that summon adventurers (and kingdom guards, if the kingdom allows it).
- **Comparative advantage (§6):** a city or kingdom can only mass-produce a trade good it has a comparative advantage in, granted by Forway for five years at a time, with price ceilings and minimum quotas. Breaking this means forfeiture and heavy fines.
- **The yearly meeting (§7):** every Paulin, the kings and city representatives meet at Forway Palace on the economy, the populace's health and threats, and must settle every disagreement before it ends.
- **Plotting (§8):** plotting against the treaty or Forway is punished harshly, up to exile to Hifuna, once proven under the Forway Law.
- **Essential goods (§9, added in 705 A.G.):** Forway manages the goods essential to the four kingdoms' safety, commonwealth and stability, with subsidies paid from taxes.

# The Forway Law

How guards and leaders in every Forway territory must deal with, arrest and punish wrongdoers.

- **Crime:** any proven harm (physical, economic or mental) a citizen suffers without consenting to it. Everyone is innocent until proven guilty. Punishments come in three sizes: minor (replaceable or recoverable within five years of contract servitude), major (irreparable, or worth more than five years of servitude) and grand (conspiracy against Forway and the Prosperity Treaty).
- **Consent:** a written contract, or a spoken declaration in front of several witnesses.
- **Punishment:** of equal or similar value: a stolen item that can't be returned is paid back including its sentimental value; bodily harm is repaid with equal harm, or comparable compensation. Local embassy leaders, village elders or Forway guard captains decide it, and kingdom rulers can override it on their own land. A citizen can be held for up to six weeks with preliminary proof, or if they might escape. For the gravest crimes, exile to Hifuna takes at least four authorities. If local courts can't agree, Forway's leaders decide at the next yearly meeting (§7).

# Gandew Common Duel Rights & Law

Trial by combat, formalized by the early Gandew Kings because spats kept turning into public brawls; historians trace the first versions back to about 375 A.G.

- **Where:** in the Low, Medium or High Pits, with at least ten witnesses.
- **Before the duel:** both duelers say why they're fighting and what they win or lose (in a marriage duel, winning means a lifelong bond; losing means they aren't meant for each other). The witnesses pick who attacks first; someone defending his honor, wife and so on gets to start on defense, and in a marriage duel the woman attacks first. The duel only starts once every witness agrees, with the duelers ten Adi apart in the middle of the pit.
- **The check:** a witness smells each dueler's whole body and tastes the junction of their Darfaries veins, between the middle and ring fingers, while they stand still, feet apart and arms down; interfering is an instant loss. Any mind or body alteration must be revealed, and everyone must agree to go on. Fleeing or asking to postpone is an automatic loss, punishable by death at the witnesses' hands.
- **The duel:** one attack per turn (a single swing, stab or punch), with physical weapons only. The attacker can move however they like; the defender can't move, but can counter without moving. Roles swap every turn, and no healing between rounds.
- **Winner and loser:** the last one alive and breathing wins, must revive the loser, and the loser must honor the terms. A loser who refuses is executed by the witnesses and handed to the winner, who can do as they please with the body, even throw it off the Goodbye Cliff.
- **King-Fest addendum:** the crown is decided by Gandew Duel, open only to natural-born Darfaries. The King is healed between duels, but he doesn't have to heal his challengers. Whoever kills the King must rip out both of the dead King's tusks to finish the transfer of power.
